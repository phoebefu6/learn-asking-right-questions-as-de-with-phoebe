Learn Asking the Right Questions as a Data Engineer with Phoebe

An operations director says: "Our numbers never match. Finance says one thing, the POS says another." It sounds like a technical request, and the data engineer who treats it as one builds a pipeline that moves the disagreement faster instead of settling which copy is real. Most of what goes wrong in data work goes wrong in the first meeting, quietly, with both sides sure they agreed. Six sessions on running that meeting as a data engineer: the six doors every discovery conversation has, why data state is the one this role opens widest, the plain second sentence that lets a business person learn how data work thinks while she answers, and a bench that puts one invented operations director with 24 hidden facts in your browser and counts what each way of asking is worth.

The six sessions 🎯 · from "our numbers never match" to a brief she can read

The running case is Harbourline, an invented chain of 38 pharmacy and clinic outlets across Singapore and Malaysia, and its operations director Adeline Tan, who has never heard the word pipeline. Every fact on her sheet is printed in the bench's bank file; the course claims the mechanism, never the numbers as facts about pharmacies.

Every system a number passes through

The till, the bank, the ledger, the spreadsheet, the phone. Who owns each, who can log in, how often each is read, and which copy gets declared real. You draw the lineage from her answers.

 🎛️ Session 3 · bench night

The engineer's questions, and the bench

Five kinds of question and the same operations director run five ways. The fast interview scores 17 and writes down "the POS is the source of truth"; the doors in order 47; data state widest, 51.

★ the bench 💬 Session 4 · core

Teaching while you ask

The one concept worth ninety seconds in an engineer's meeting: most mismatches are timing, not truth. Twelve teach-back lines rewritten for a person who has never heard of a pipeline.

 🌱 Session 5 · core

Three projects, starting with one real copy

Her pains become three candidates, each starting by declaring one copy real, and the winner is a written month-end bridge from the export the bookkeeper already sends: no new tool, no admin login, an answer for the auditor.

 📄 Session 6 · capstone

The mock meeting and the one-page brief

A full run on the bench with your own sheet, scored, then the brief she can read: the lineage as five boxes, the one real copy, the auditor's date. Final scorecard.

Four chairs at the same table 🪑

The same framework, the same company and the same bench engine, built four times for four roles. Each course marks a different door to open widest and carries its own question bank and fact sheet. The framework itself lives in the data analyst course's session 1; this course links to it.

As a data analyst ↗Widest door: decision. Who does what with the number, and by when. As a data scientist ↗Widest door: outcome. What to predict, what a wrong guess costs, how often it happens today. The human workflow today, the error you can tolerate, who checks the output.
